# Front Loop Only (FLO)

Working into the front loop only means inserting your hook under just the forward-facing strand of a stitch rather than both strands. This creates a horizontal ridge on the surface of your fabric and leaves the back loop free for a second pass of stitches later.

---

## Anatomy of a Stitch

When you look at the top of a completed stitch, you see a V shape formed by two strands:
- **Front loop** — the strand closest to you (facing outward)
- **Back loop** — the strand furthest from you (facing inward)

Normally you insert your hook under both loops. FLO means you only pick up the front one.

---

## What FLO Creates

**A ridge on the right side:** Because the back loop is left open, it pops forward slightly, forming a clean horizontal line across the fabric. This ridge is visible from outside the piece.

**A foundation for extra stitches:** Since the back loop is untouched, you can come back and work into it later to add a ruffle, a fin, a color trim, or other decorative elements.

---

## Step-by-Step

**1. Reach the stitch where your pattern says "FLO" or "in front loop only"**

**2. Insert your hook under the front loop only**
Look at the top of the next stitch. Position your hook so it passes only under the strand facing you, not under the back strand.

**3. Complete your stitch as normal**
Yarn over, pull through (one loop), yarn over, pull through two loops — standard single crochet, just using only one of the two strands at the top.

**4. Continue across the round**
Every stitch in an FLO round is done this way.

---

## Common Uses in Amigurumi

- **Color change joins:** Working the first round after a color change in BLO or FLO makes the transition line neater
- **Neck definition:** A round of FLO creates a visual dividing line between head and body
- **Skirt and ruffle bases:** Work a round in FLO, then come back and sc into the unused back loops to add a ruffle layer
- **Ear bases:** Some patterns use FLO to define where an ear folds

---

## Tips

- **Go slow the first time** — it's easy to accidentally catch both loops until your eye adjusts to picking just one.
- **Your hook angle matters:** A slight outward tilt of the hook tip helps it slide under just the front loop cleanly.
- **Mark the FLO round** on your pattern so you remember to come back to the back loops if needed.
