# Single Crochet (sc)

The single crochet is the backbone of amigurumi. It creates a tight, dense fabric that holds stuffing without gaps showing through. If you can single crochet, you can make almost any plushie.

---

## What You Need

- Yarn and crochet hook
- A starting chain or magic ring (from your pattern)

---

## Step-by-Step

**1. Start from a foundation**
Your pattern will tell you to either chain a certain number of stitches or start from a magic ring. For most amigurumi, you start from a magic ring with 6 stitches.

**2. Insert your hook**
Push the hook through the center of the next stitch (or the top of the chain). The hook goes under both loops of the stitch unless your pattern says otherwise.

**3. Yarn over**
With the working yarn, wrap the yarn over the hook from back to front. You now have two loops on your hook.

**4. Pull through the stitch**
Draw the yarn through the stitch. You now have two loops on the hook.

**5. Yarn over again**
Wrap the yarn over the hook one more time.

**6. Pull through both loops**
Draw the yarn through both loops on the hook. One loop remains — this is your completed single crochet.

**7. Continue across the round**
Move to the next stitch and repeat steps 2–6 until you reach the end of the round.

---

## Working in the Round vs. Rows

Most amigurumi work in a **continuous spiral** — you never join rounds, you just keep going. Place a stitch marker in the last stitch of each round so you know when to count.

If a pattern uses **joined rounds**, you slip stitch to join at the end of each round, then chain 1 before the next round begins. The chain-1 does NOT count as a stitch in single crochet.

---

## Tips

- **Count your stitches at the end of every round.** Missing or adding even one stitch will throw off the shape of your plushie.
- **Don't crochet too tightly.** If your hook struggles to enter the stitch, loosen your tension slightly — tight fabric is good, but you still need to work comfortably.
- **The "right side" of single crochet** has a V shape on each stitch. When working in the round, keep this side facing out (toward you).
- **Use a smaller hook** than what the yarn label suggests. For amigurumi, going one hook size down closes up gaps so stuffing doesn't show through.
