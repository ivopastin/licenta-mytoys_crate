# Invisible Decrease (dec)

The invisible decrease (also called the "magic decrease" or "sc2tog through front loops") combines two stitches into one. It's the technique that closes up your amigurumi shapes — turning a dome into a closed sphere. Done correctly, it leaves no holes or bumps.

---

## Why "Invisible"?

The standard single crochet two-together (sc2tog) method can leave a gap between stitches, which is especially visible on amigurumi bodies. The **invisible decrease** eliminates this gap by only picking up the front loops of the next two stitches before completing the stitch.

---

## Step-by-Step

**1. Identify the next two stitches**
You're going to combine them into one stitch.

**2. Insert your hook into the FRONT loop only of the first stitch**
Do not go through both loops — only the front loop. Leave the back loop untouched.

**3. Without finishing the stitch, insert your hook into the FRONT loop only of the SECOND stitch**
You now have three loops on your hook: two front loops from the two stitches, plus the loop already on your hook from before.

**4. Yarn over and pull through both front loops**
Draw the yarn through the two front loops together. Two loops remain on your hook.

**5. Yarn over and pull through both remaining loops**
Draw the yarn through the final two loops. One loop remains — invisible decrease complete. You have combined two stitches into one.

---

## How It Closes a Shape

For a standard 6-stitch base sphere, the decrease rounds mirror the increase rounds in reverse:

- \*sc 2, dec\* repeat around (removes 6 stitches per round)
- \*sc 1, dec\* repeat around
- dec around
- Pull remaining stitches closed with yarn tail

---

## Tips

- **The front-loop-only approach is everything.** If you accidentally go through both loops of one stitch, you'll see a gap. Always only front loops for both.
- **Decreases are firmer than increases** — they pull the fabric inward, so your tension may feel tighter. Keep your hook movement relaxed.
- **Don't decrease too fast at the end.** Rushing the final closure leaves a lumpy seam. Follow the pattern's dec schedule precisely.
- **Stuff BEFORE closing** — once the opening gets to about 4–6 stitches, stop and fill your plushie with stuffing before completing the decrease rounds.
- **Close the last stitches** by cutting yarn, threading the tail through a tapestry needle, and running it through the front loops of the remaining stitches. Pull tight and weave in the end.
