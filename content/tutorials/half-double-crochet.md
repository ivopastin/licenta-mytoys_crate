# Half Double Crochet (hdc)

The half double crochet sits in height between a single crochet and a double crochet. It has a unique characteristic: a third loop appears on the back of each stitch, which many designers use for texture and detail effects in amigurumi.

---

## What You Need

- Yarn and crochet hook
- A foundation chain or stitches to work into

---

## Step-by-Step

**1. Yarn over first**
Just like double crochet, wrap the working yarn over the hook before inserting it anywhere. One wrap.

**2. Insert your hook into the stitch**
Push the hook into the next stitch. Three strands are now managed by your hook.

**3. Yarn over and pull through the stitch**
Wrap the yarn and draw it through only the stitch. You now have three loops on your hook.

**4. Yarn over one final time**
Wrap the yarn over the hook once more.

**5. Pull through all three loops at once**
Draw the yarn through ALL three loops on your hook simultaneously. One loop remains — half double crochet complete!

This "pull through all three at once" is what distinguishes it from double crochet (which does two separate pull-throughs).

---

## The Third Loop

When you look at the back of a half double crochet stitch, you'll see a horizontal strand that runs behind the stitch. This is called the **third loop**. 

Working into this third loop on the following round creates a very neat horizontal ridge — used in some amigurumi patterns to define a "neck" area or to add decorative borders.

---

## Tips

- **hdc creates a slightly thicker, denser fabric than dc** but is more flexible and softer than sc. Good for floppy ears, tails, and body sections where you want some give.
- **The turning chain for hdc is chain 2.** This chain-2 does not count as a stitch in most patterns (unlike dc where chain-3 usually counts).
- **Tension is important**: if your three-loop pull-through is too tight, the stitch will look lumpy. Practice keeping a relaxed hook movement through all three.
