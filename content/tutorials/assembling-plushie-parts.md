# Assembling Plushie Parts

You've crocheted the head, the body, the ears, the arms, and the legs. Now comes the step that turns a pile of crocheted blobs into an actual character: sewing everything together. Clean assembly is what separates a professional-looking plushie from a lumpy one.

---

## What You Need

- All finished, stuffed parts
- Tapestry needle (blunt tip, large eye)
- The yarn tails left when finishing each part (or a separate length of matching yarn)
- Pins or stitch markers for positioning
- Scissors

---

## Before You Sew: Position Everything First

Don't start sewing until you're happy with how the pieces look. Lay the body flat and arrange all the parts around it. Step back and look at the overall shape. Consider:

- **Arms:** How high on the body? Pointed down, forward, or out?
- **Legs:** How far apart? How far from the bottom of the body?
- **Ears:** On top of the head? Slightly to the sides?
- **Tail:** Center back? Slightly off-center for a more playful look?

Use pins to temporarily hold pieces in place while you assess. Once you're satisfied, start sewing.

---

## The Ladder Stitch

The ladder stitch is the standard technique for sewing amigurumi parts. It creates an almost invisible seam.

**Step 1:** Thread your tapestry needle with a length of matching yarn (or use the part's yarn tail).

**Step 2:** Anchor your yarn by weaving into the body piece a few stitches to hide the knot.

**Step 3:** Insert the needle into the edge of the part (the arm, leg, ear, etc.) and pull through.

**Step 4:** Bring the needle across to the body and insert it into a stitch on the body surface, pulling through.

**Step 5:** Bring the needle back to the part and insert it one stitch along from where you last exited.

**Step 6:** Alternate back and forth — part, body, part, body — working your way around the opening of the piece.

**Step 7:** Once you've gone all the way around, pull the yarn firmly. The two edges will draw together and the "rungs of the ladder" will disappear inside. The seam becomes nearly invisible.

---

## Sewing Flat Parts (Ears, Fins, Wings)

Flat pieces like ears are sewn slightly differently since they don't have a stuffed opening — just a flat bottom edge.

**1.** Hold or pin the ear in position on the head.

**2.** Thread your needle through the **bottom edge of the ear** and **into the corresponding row of the head**.

**3.** Work back and forth along the base of the ear, catching the head fabric with each pass.

**4.** Pull snug, knot, and weave in the end inside the head.

Tip: For floppy ears, only sew the front half of the base and leave the back half free — the ear will hang naturally.

---

## Sewing Arms and Legs

Arms and legs are typically attached in one of two ways depending on the pattern:

**Fixed attachment:** Sewn flat against the body. The limb doesn't move. Use the ladder stitch around the full opening.

**Jointed attachment (poseable):** Use plastic joints or the "button joint" technique — thread yarn through the body and both limbs with a long needle, looping several times, then pull taut and knot. The limbs can be repositioned but held firmly.

---

## Hiding Yarn Ends

After sewing, you'll have yarn tails left over. Never just cut them close to the surface.

**The right way:**
1. Thread the tail onto your tapestry needle.
2. Insert the needle through the body of the plushie and out the other side (not through the fabric surface — through the stuffing cavity).
3. Give the yarn a gentle tug and snip close to the exit point. The elasticity of the stuffing will pull the small tail inside, hiding it completely.

---

## Tips

- **Use matching yarn** for sewing. Even if your stitching isn't perfect, matching color will hide it completely.
- **Sew tighter than you think you need to.** Plushies are handled and squeezed; a loose attachment will loosen further over time.
- **Arms positioned slightly forward** (not straight out to the sides) look more natural and huggable.
- **Stuff extremities firmly** — floppy arms and legs look unfinished. Add extra stuffing to the tips of limbs before sewing closed.
- **A positioning trick:** if you're unsure about arm height, hold the assembled (but not yet sewn) plushie and see where the arms naturally fall when you rest it on a flat surface. That's usually the right height.
