# Back Loop Only (BLO)

Working into the back loop only means inserting your hook under only the rear-facing strand of a stitch. Like FLO, it creates a textural ridge — but the ridge appears on the inside of the work, and the visual line shows on the outside as a clean, raised edge.

---

## Front Loop vs. Back Loop

When looking at the top of a stitch:
- **Front loop** — the strand facing toward you
- **Back loop** — the strand facing away from you

BLO means you skip the front loop and insert only under the back one.

---

## What BLO Creates

**A ridge on the outside:** Because the front loop is left open (facing outward), it raises slightly and creates a visible horizontal line from the right side of the piece — often more prominent than the FLO ridge.

**The unused front loops:** These can be worked into later to add decorative layers, or left alone — they simply create the visual texture.

---

## Step-by-Step

**1. Reach the stitch where your pattern says "BLO"**

**2. Insert your hook under the back loop only**
Tilt your hook slightly inward and thread it under only the strand furthest from you.

**3. Complete the stitch as normal**
Yarn over, pull up a loop through the back loop, yarn over, pull through both loops — single crochet done, just using one loop.

**4. Continue across the round**
Every stitch in a BLO round works this way.

---

## Common Uses in Amigurumi

- **Foot/sole separation:** A round of BLO at the bottom of a leg cleanly separates the sole from the leg shaft
- **Brim and hat edges:** BLO rounds on hat brims create a very clean fold line
- **Joined color sections:** Working the first round of a new color in BLO produces a sharp, professional-looking color join
- **Jeans or pants details:** A BLO round in a contrasting color mimics a waistband or belt line on a doll's clothing

---

## Tips

- **BLO stitches can feel tighter** because you're working one strand instead of two. If your tension increases noticeably, loosen your grip slightly.
- **The ridge is directional** — it will appear on the side facing out when the work is turned, so think about which side of the fabric you want the ridge on before deciding between FLO and BLO.
- **Label which round is BLO in your pattern notes** — it's easy to forget after ripping and restarting.
