# Attaching Safety Eyes

Safety eyes are the plastic button eyes used on amigurumi and stuffed toys. They have a post that passes through the fabric and a washer that locks from behind — once locked, they're permanent. Placement makes all the difference: the same plushie can look curious, sleepy, or alarmed depending on where the eyes sit.

---

## What You Need

- Safety eyes (size 6mm–12mm for most amigurumi, larger for bigger plushies)
- The crocheted head piece (NOT yet stuffed — or only lightly stuffed so you can reach inside)
- A tapestry needle or the crochet hook tip for marking placement

---

## When to Insert Eyes

**Before stuffing.** This is non-negotiable. The washers lock from inside the head, and once the head is stuffed and closed, you cannot reach in to attach the washers. Insert and lock both eyes before you stuff and close the head.

---

## Step-by-Step

**1. Mark potential positions with pins or stitch markers**
Place pins where you're thinking the eyes should go. Hold the head at arm's length and look at it from different angles. The face will feel very different depending on:
- Vertical position (higher = surprised, lower = calm)
- Horizontal spacing (wider = cute/young, closer = mature/focused)
- How many rows below the top of the head

**2. Count stitches for symmetry**
Once you're happy with one eye position, count the stitches and rows to the center of the face. Mirror the count for the second eye. For example: if the right eye is 3 rows below the top and 4 stitches to the right of center, the left eye should be 3 rows below the top and 4 stitches to the left of center.

**3. Push the post through the fabric**
Find the stitch where you want the eye. Use your hook or needle tip to gently widen the space between stitches (don't cut any yarn!). Push the eye post through from the outside, between the strands of the stitch.

**4. Attach the washer from inside**
Reach inside the head. Place the washer over the post (the flat side of the washer faces inward) and push down firmly until it clicks. This may require some force — that click means it's locked.

**5. Test the eye is secure**
Try to pull the eye back out. It should not move. A properly locked safety eye is extremely difficult to remove — handle with care around young children if needed.

**6. Repeat for the second eye**
Use your stitch count reference to position the second eye symmetrically, then lock it.

---

## Eye Size Guide

| Plushie Size | Recommended Eye Size |
|---|---|
| Tiny (under 4cm) | 4mm–6mm |
| Small (5–10cm) | 6mm–8mm |
| Medium (10–15cm) | 9mm–12mm |
| Large (15cm+) | 12mm–18mm |

---

## Tips

- **Live with the position for a few minutes** before locking. Set the plushie down, walk away, come back. First impressions after a short break are more reliable than staring at it for 10 minutes straight.
- **Asymmetric eyes can add character** — but only by 1–2 stitches. Too much asymmetry just looks like a mistake.
- **Black safety eyes are the most versatile** for amigurumi. Colored eyes (blue, green, brown) work for dolls. Eyes with white pupils add a lot of life.
- **Use a backing button** on very soft or loosely crocheted fabric — place a small flat button on the inside before the washer to distribute the pressure over more fabric.
- **Never use craft googly eyes as a replacement** for safety eyes on toys that might be chewed — the disc pops off easily and is a choking hazard.
