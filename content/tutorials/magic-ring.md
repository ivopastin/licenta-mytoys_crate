# The Magic Ring

The magic ring (also called a magic circle or adjustable ring) is the starting point for almost every amigurumi project. Unlike a chain start, it creates a center that you can pull completely closed — no hole left behind.

---

## What You Need

- Your yarn
- A crochet hook (size matching your yarn weight)
- Your fingers!

---

## Step-by-Step

**1. Form the loop**
Drape the yarn over two fingers, with the tail hanging behind. The working yarn (connected to the ball) goes over your index finger, and the tail hangs down between your fingers.

**2. Cross the tail over the working yarn**
Bring the tail over the top of the working yarn to form an X shape on your fingers. This crossing is what makes the ring adjustable.

**3. Insert your hook**
Slide your hook under the strand closest to your fingertips (the one connected to the ball) and through the center of the loop.

**4. Yarn over and pull through**
Yarn over with the working yarn and draw it through the loop on your hook. You now have one loop on your hook — this is your first chain to secure the ring.

**5. Chain 1 (this does not count as a stitch)**
Yarn over and pull through the loop. This chain keeps the ring stable while you work into it.

**6. Work your first round of stitches**
Insert your hook into the center of the ring (not into the chain — directly into the ring opening), yarn over, pull up a loop, and complete your stitch as normal. Repeat until you have as many stitches as your pattern calls for.

**7. Pull the tail to close**
Gently tug on the yarn tail. You will feel one loop tighten. Then tug the tail again — the ring will close completely. No hole!

**8. Slip stitch to join (if working in joined rounds)**
Insert the hook into the first stitch, yarn over, pull through both loops to join the round. If your pattern says "work in a continuous spiral," skip the join and keep going.

---

## Tips

- **Hold the ring between your thumb and middle finger** as you work — this keeps it from twisting.
- **Don't panic if it feels loose at first.** Once you complete a few stitches and pull the tail, the ring firms up nicely.
- **If the ring keeps slipping open**, try wrapping the yarn around your fingers one extra time before forming the loop.
- **Mark your first stitch** with a stitch marker — it's easy to lose track when working into a magic ring.
- Left-handed? Mirror all the directions — the ring forms the same way, just on your opposite hand.
